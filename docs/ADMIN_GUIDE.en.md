# Admin Guide

Guide for the administrator of a city in **Historic City Map** (first city: **București dispărut**, Bucharest). This is the outline of the guide: sections are filled in together with the features, in milestones M2–M5. Romanian version: [ADMIN_GUIDE.ro.md](ADMIN_GUIDE.ro.md); both versions change together.

> Status (M6): sign-in, the Setup Assistant, the daily editors below (Home, photos, places, layers, settings, history, jobs, admins and crash reports), the historical imagery (search USGS, import a free frame, the automatic georeferencing with its suggested pairs, Approve, the compute-unit guard, the free Studio upload, a GeoTIFF upload, the georeferencer, crop, replace), buying a scan (the order card, I ordered it, the daily watch, the reminders and the import on delivery), the notifications (the inbox, and push on a device you turn it on for) and the editors' assists (title suggestions from OpenStreetMap, Romanian and English drafts) work end to end against the Firebase emulators and the fake Google, Mapbox, USGS, OpenStreetMap and Vertex AI services of the tests, and what is published reaches the public viewer, whose swipe compare shows two layers side by side; the raster pipeline itself was run against the same fakes on a synthetic frame. Since M5 one project holds several cities (the city menu, New city, Publishing, roles per city, each city's budget and its static export), each closed to the admins of the others, and a city made in the admin gets its own public site; the tests take a new city from New city to its public site and to an export that runs with no Firebase. On the real cloud it waits for the server's deploy, Provision turning on the Vertex AI and FCM APIs, and your first real scan purchase (`docs/research/M4-status.md`, `docs/research/M5-status.md`). The first real project is set up with Claude, with your yes at each step. Since M6 the Setup Assistant needs no terminal and no Claude after the admin's first deploy: **Install the server**, the imagery pipeline and **Publish the map** are buttons, run by Google Cloud Build in your project with your Google permission; the public map is at `https://<project>.web.app/` and the admin at `https://<project>.web.app/admin/` (`docs/research/M6-status.md`).

## What you do and what the app does

The app does almost everything automatically. Only you can:

- create accounts and enter billing or card details (Google Cloud, Mapbox, USGS);
- accept terms and approve Google sign-in consents;
- paste the two secret keys, once: the Mapbox secret token and the USGS application token;
- pay for USGS scan orders, if you want frames that are not scanned yet: the app opens the scene's own EarthExplorer page, you buy the scan there, come back and click **I ordered it**; the rest is automatic.

The app opens the exact page (deep link), shows you the button, verifies the result and moves on by itself. Secret keys go straight to Secret Manager; they are not kept in the browser and never shown again after saving.

## First sign-in

- **A new map (no server yet)**: before billing is linked the map has no server, so the first steps talk to Google directly from your browser ("start-up mode"). Its first deploy (the admin alone, before the server, at `https://<project>.web.app/admin/`; the root says the map is being set up) says so, so the page does not even try the server; it is the one deploy outside the app, done once with your yes. An old address of the admin at the root (`/#/setup`, a bookmark) forwards to `/admin/`. Sign in with Google: Google asks you to allow read-only access to your Cloud projects, and the app checks with Google that your account owns the project. Billing, preparing the project and installing the server follow (below); as soon as the server answers, you become the map's owner automatically, without another click.
- **A map that already has a server but no owner**, for example when you come back later: the app offers **Confirm with Google**: Google asks for the same read-only access, and the app checks that your account owns the project. You become the map's owner; no e-mail address is configured anywhere.
- **Google's permission screens**: each step asks only for the access it needs, at the moment it runs (read-only at sign-in, billing for the budget, Google Cloud for preparing the project, Google Analytics for one setting); daily work asks for none. The app says why before each one. Google may say that it has not verified the app: that is expected, the app belongs to your own project; open the advanced options and continue. The access stays in the browser tab only, for at most an hour, and is never saved; when it expires, the step asks you to continue.
- Anyone else needs an invitation: an admin enters the person's address, the app keeps only a fingerprint of it (not the address) and gives the link to send. The invited person signs in with Google at that link and chooses **Join**. An invitation expires after 14 days.
- A Google account without an invitation is told so and signed out.

## Setup Assistant

The first screen until every required card is green; **Check again** re-checks the whole page or one card at any time; on the whole page it also lists the **Server checks**, each with its state, and a failing one says why and links the step that fixes it. The page shows a progress ring ("6 of 11 steps done"; "8 of 11 steps done, 1 left for later" once USGS waits) and one card per step, lettered a to i (d3: the imagery pipeline). Once every required card is done the header says so ("Setup is done"; with USGS left for later or waiting for its approval, it says that too) and an empty address opens Home. Each card has a status chip with an icon (**To do**, **Waiting**, **Done**, **Later**, **Needs attention**, **Unavailable now**; while the step works, the loading indicator takes the chip's place; right after the page opens, until the step's first check answers, the chip reads **Checking…** and the header "Checking every step…"), why the step is needed and what it costs, the deep links to the provider's pages (each opens in a new tab), the Google permission note before any Google window, the step's own button and **Check again**. On a phone the cards form one column and the open card expands in place; from 840 px wide the list sits on the left and the open step beside it. When a step turns green the next one opens by itself (the analytics card stays open for its stream setting).

The whole assistant takes at most eleven clicks or fields and four Google permissions (nine with USGS left for later), and no terminal command; a button clicked more than an hour after Google's last permission asks for it once more; what you do on Google's, Mapbox's and USGS's own pages is extra and only yours. Screenshots of every step, at phone, tablet, laptop and large-screen widths in light and dark, come from the end-to-end tests ([TESTING.md](TESTING.md) → *Review screenshots*).

**Start-up mode.** Until the map's server is installed, a banner says "Start-up mode: the map has no server yet, so these steps talk to Google directly." and the navigation offers Setup only. What the steps find is kept in this browser tab (never a token, an address or a card detail) and survives a reload; the server records it once it is installed.

### a. Sign-in and ownership

- **Sign in with Google** opens Google's window with read-only access to Google Cloud; Google then confirms that your account owns the project. Done. The sign-in stays in this tab (a reload keeps it; a new tab asks again), so the public map, which shares the address, can never use it.
- If the Resource Manager service is off in a brand-new project, the sign-in panel offers to enable it (one click, the Google Cloud permission).

### b. Google Cloud billing and budget

- **Link billing and create the 5 USD budget**: one click and one Google permission (billing; Google Cloud instead when the billing services must be switched on first, and then step c asks for nothing more). The app links your billing account to the project (the Blaze plan) and creates the monthly budget in the account's currency (5 USD, 5 EUR, 4 GBP, 25 RON) with alerts at 50%, 90% and 100% of the spending. The card states the cost before the click: about USD 0.5–2 a month.
- **No billing account yet**: the card says so and offers **Create a billing account**, which opens Google's page for a new account, where you enter the card. The card then says "Waiting for the new billing account…" and that the page checks every 10 seconds and goes on by itself (for 30 minutes, while the tab is visible); **Link billing and create the 5 USD budget** stays off while there is nothing to link, and the new account is linked by itself.
- **A new account is most likely a free trial** (Google's 300 USD for 90 days): the card says so, and Home reminds you about two weeks before its end to upgrade it in Google Cloud, or the map's server stops then; **It is a paid account** dismisses the reminder. An account you had before Setup is never guessed to be one: if it is a trial, choose **This account is a free trial** on the linked card (one click) and Home reminds you the same way.
- **Several accounts**: choose one (the first is preselected) and **Link this account**.

### c. Prepare the Google Cloud project

- **Prepare the project**: one click (and the Google Cloud permission, unless billing asked for it already). The app prepares, in order, the 16 items listed on the card, each with its state: the Google services, the service accounts (with the installer's account, which Cloud Build runs the installations as), the permissions (with the right of the server's accounts to receive events and scheduled calls, the Editor role taken away from the App Engine default account, which nothing of the map runs as, and the Compute Engine default account, which Google builds the server's functions as, given the narrow Cloud Build role in place of Editor, with the installer allowed to act as it), the Artifact Registry repository of the imagery pipeline, the storage buckets and their browser access and clean-up, the places for the secret keys, the publishing queue, keyless installation from GitHub, App Check, the budget alerts, the organisation's policies, e-mail on new crashes and the browser key's restriction. Running it again changes nothing that is in place.
- A Google service switched on a moment ago may answer as if it were off for a few minutes: the card waits for it ("Google is still switching on …") instead of failing. An item that fails says why and the items that need it wait ("Waiting: …"); the others go on. **Retry what failed** repeats only those (one click each time).
- "The periodic check" waits with "Created by the first server installation."; that does not hold the step back.

### d. Installing the map's server

- **Install the server**: one click (and Google's permission once more if Prepare the project was more than an hour ago). The app hands the installation to Google Cloud Build in your project, which runs it as the installer's account from files published with this admin and checked by their fingerprint: the database and storage rules, then every function of the server. It takes about 15 minutes; the card states the build minutes it uses against Cloud Build's free minutes of the month. It needs only the items of Prepare the project the server uses: one that only the imagery pipeline needs (its Artifact Registry repository) does not hold it back.
- The card shows the installation's steps (download and check the files, prepare the Firebase tools, install the server's packages, the rules, the functions, check every function), the one under way, and once how many functions are installed. A brand-new project's event service may still be getting ready: the card says "Google is still preparing … ; we try again at …" and the installation retries by itself.
- An installation your last visit left at work is shown with when it was last seen ("Last seen installing on … Check again to see now."), never as running, until the card has looked at Google Cloud Build again (by itself while Google's permission is fresh, else with **Check again**); it says "Checking the installation…" only while that look runs, then shows where the build is now. If Google does not answer, the card keeps the installation as last seen, with Google's error, and looks again by itself. **Update the map** waits for a look that answers. "Running for N min." counts from when the build started work; a build still waiting for Google Cloud Build shows no time.
- If the admin itself was updated while this tab was open, a banner above the page says "A new version of the admin is ready" with **Reload** (you stay signed in); until you reload, an installation refuses to start and asks for the reload.
- A stopped installation says at which step, shows the last lines of its log (secrets removed) and a link to the build in the Google Cloud console; **Try again** starts one new installation, also for an update that stopped once Setup is done. A server newer than this admin is never replaced, even with a function missing: the card asks you to reload the page.
- The card turns green by itself once every function is installed and answers. When this admin is newer than what is installed, or the server is only partly there (one from before this release), the cards and Home say "An update is ready" with **Update the map**: one click updates the server, then the imagery pipeline and the public map. Setup stays done while an update runs, and taking over the map (d2) is never asked again. Every build of the admin is an update over an earlier build, even when the version number is the same (1.0.0 and 1.0.0): the app orders builds by the time of the code they were built from, and it never replaces a server built later than itself with an older admin (it says the server is newer and asks you to reload instead). Google's note about the access it asks for appears only beside a button that needs it (Install, Publish, Try again, Update the map, Continue); a card with nothing to do shows none.
- **Optional: install from GitHub Actions** (collapsed): the repository variables, each with a copy button, for an owner who wants every release installed by GitHub instead.

### d3. The imagery pipeline (optional)

- Installed by the same click as the server, with nothing to fill in: Cloud Build builds the pipeline's image into the project's repository and creates its three jobs (a small one, 1 vCPU, that downloads the scenes, and two that process them). Its card shows the steps and, if it stops, the step, the log and **Try again**; the server is not affected. Missing beside an installed server (installed from GitHub, or held up at the server's click), it has its own **Install the pipeline**. Since the release of 2026-11-01 the installation names Google's Docker builder by its fingerprint, so the button is there; an older admin's card says the installation from the app comes with a later release (the map works without the pipeline meanwhile).

### d2. Taking over the map

- As soon as the server answers, the app makes you the map's owner with the Google permission it still holds (no click), creates the city from the old site's data, records what start-up mode found and leaves start-up mode: the banner goes and the navigation shows Setup, Admins and Crashes. The Mapbox card opens.
- If the Google permission has expired (more than an hour, or the page was reloaded), the claim panel asks for one click: **Confirm with Google**.

### e. Mapbox

- Create the account and leave the demo plan on Mapbox's pages (card and billing address; the card's link says to choose *Rotate token*), tick the usage alerts, then create the secret token `historic-map-server` with the scopes the card lists (**Copy the scopes**).
- Paste the token into **Secret token (sk.…)**: a secret token is saved at once, with no other click (a public `pk.` token is explained instead and nothing is sent; a token typed by hand goes with Enter or **Save**, after the card says "This looks like a secret token."). The token goes to Secret Manager once, and the app creates the public tokens of the site and the admin, restricted to their addresses (the public map's on `https://<project>.web.app` and `https://<project>.firebaseapp.com`; the server makes it again by itself when those addresses change, and says so on the card if Mapbox refuses).
- **Replace the visitors' token** and **Replace the admin's token** (once Mapbox is saved) make a new public token after a confirmation; the card says when the old one is deleted (ten minutes later, so open pages keep working). Use it when a token leaked.
- A demo account cannot create tokens: the card says "Mapbox did not create the public token. Check the billing details of the Mapbox account." with the link to add the payment method; add it on Mapbox's page and paste the token again.

### f. USGS

- Register on ERS and request M2M access with the pre-written answers and request text (**Copy the request text**); approval takes 24–48 business hours.
- Type the **ERS username** (and, optionally, the token's expiry date), then **Open the USGS token page**: the paste box opens first and a 60-second countdown starts ("Paste the token within 60 s"), because USGS shows a new token for about a minute. Paste it: it is checked and saved at once, without a Save click. If the time runs out, the card says so and the box stays open: create a new token.
- Before approval the card is **Waiting** with "USGS has not approved M2M access for this account yet. We check again every 6 hours.", since when it has been waiting, and the next check's time. Setup still counts as done ("Setup is done; the historical imagery search starts once USGS approves M2M access.") and Home reminds you with the date. The server notices the approval on its own; the card is green the next time you open the admin.
- **Do it later** (one click): USGS only brings the historical imagery, so the step can wait. Its chip says **Later**, Setup is done without it, and Home shows "USGS is not set up yet: historical imagery search is off." with **Set up USGS** until you do it; the Historical imagery page asks for USGS first meanwhile.

### g. Analytics and crash reports (optional)

- Follow the link to the Firebase console and enable Google Analytics for the project (you accept Google's terms there). The card then checks every 15 seconds for 15 minutes and shows the measurement id.
- **Stop counting address changes as page views**: one click and the Google Analytics permission; the map rewrites its address on every move, so the stream's history page views must stay off. The card then says "Address changes are no longer counted as page views."
- **Create the measurement reports**: one click (and the Google Analytics permission, if the page holds none from the last hour). The app finds the Google Analytics property whose stream has the measurement id shown and registers there, once, what the performance measurements below need for GA4's reports: the custom dimensions `metric_name`, `metric_rating`, `debug_target`, `window_class`, `release`, `phase`, `step`, `outcome`, `area`, `target`, `error_kind` and the custom metrics `metric_value` and `duration_ms` (milliseconds) and `error_count`. The card says how many it created and how many were there already; clicking again creates nothing. If your Google account cannot edit that property, the card says so: sign in with its owner (or give your account the Editor role in Google Analytics).
- **E-mail me about new crashes** is on by default; switching it asks for the Google Cloud permission.
- The **Crash reports** panel (also the **Crashes** page) lists the error groups of the last seven days.
- Visitors are measured only after they consent; Google Analytics never receives the address's hash, which holds the visitor's view and position.
- **Performance measurements** (since the release of 2026-10-31): with the same consent, the public map and the admin also send how fast they are and where they fail, so they can be improved: the Core Web Vitals of every page (`web_vitals`: LCP, INP, CLS, FCP, TTFB, with the element of the app concerned), the map's start (`viewer_timing`: the manifest, the first render, the map complete), a photo's opening and swipe compare's (`photo_timing`, `compare_timing`), a map that cannot load (`map_unavailable`) and the map's errors counted by kind (`map_errors`); in the admin, the time to the signed-in screen (`admin_ready`), each Setup step's check (`setup_check`), Provision per item (`provision_item`), each installation build (`install_build`: duration and the failing step, never its log), each save (`editor_save`) and the jobs you watched finish (`job_done`). Never the map's position, a coordinate, an e-mail address, a name or a text. The full list: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` → *Field measurements*. Update your city's privacy text (Settings) to say so; a new city's text already does.
- **Seeing them**: in Google Analytics, **Reports → Engagement → Events** lists them by name after a day. For the numbers, the parameters must be registered once under **Admin → Custom definitions**: **Create the measurement reports** (above) does it (custom dimensions `metric_name`, `metric_rating`, `debug_target`, `window_class`, `release`, `phase`, `step`, `outcome`, `area`, `target`, `error_kind`, and custom metrics `metric_value` (milliseconds; CLS without a unit), `duration_ms` (milliseconds), `error_count`); you can also add them there by hand. Then **Explore → Free form**: event name `web_vitals`, rows `metric_name` and `window_class`, values the average (or, after a BigQuery export, the 75th percentile) of `metric_value`, filtered by `release` to compare two releases.

### h. The old Bucharest photos (optional)

- **Import the photos (57)**: one click. The 57 are the old site's 45 photos and the 12 it gained in 2023. The photos go through the normal processing three at a time ("Photos imported: N of 57"), are published once processed and stay in the rights-review queue; the card ends with "All the photos are on the map (57)." (also said at the bottom of the page once Setup has moved on) and the city's public data lists them. A photo whose upload event did not reach the server is processed again by itself. Importing again, or after a closed tab, adds nothing twice and finishes only what is missing.
- **Import the missing photos (n)**: a map that already has some of them (the 45 imported before the 2023 additions came, or an import that stopped) says how many it lacks; one click imports only those. The photos already on the map stay as you left them: one you deleted is not brought back, one you unpublished stays unpublished, your texts are not touched.
- **Add the old site's directions (n)**: the old site later gave some photos a direction (the way the camera faced) they lack on your map; Parc Ferdinand is one, and the old site also moved its point a little. The card says how many ("Directions the old site adds to photos without one: n"); one click adds them. A photo you aimed or moved yourself keeps your direction and place. The same click adds to the credits the photographers they do not name yet (Studiourile Buftea, of the 2023 photos). Clicking again changes nothing.
- A photo that could not be processed is listed with its reason; the others are imported.

### i. Publish the map

- **Publish the map**: one click (and Google's permission when the page's Google Cloud permission is more than an hour old; the Google Analytics permission of step g no longer replaces it). Each publish moves part of Hosting's free daily transfer: about 60 MB while the old photos (step h) are not all imported, about 7 MB once they are, because the publish then leaves their originals out of the site (step h no longer needs them; publishing before the import is complete keeps them). Cloud Build publishes the public map on the project's Firebase Hosting site: the first city at `https://<project>.web.app/`, every other published city at `https://<project>.web.app/<id>/`, the admin staying at `/admin/`. The button is off, with the reason, until a city is published (import the old photos first, step h, so visitors see them). Once published the card says "The map is public." with **Open the public map**; Hosting keeps the last ten versions.
- Later releases of this admin: the card says "An update is ready" and **Update the map** publishes the new version after the server's update. Publishing on GitHub Pages instead is optional and needs the repository (a developer's step).

## Daily work

The navigation holds Home, Setup, Photos, Places, Layers, Historical imagery, Jobs, Notifications, Settings, Admins, History and Crashes (on a phone the bar keeps Setup, Photos, Places and Layers, the rest is under **More**; Notifications, or More when it holds it, carries the number of unread notifications). Every editor works the same way:

- **Lists and editors.** From 840 px wide the list sits on the left and the open item beside it; on a tablet the item replaces the list, with **Back**; on a phone it fills the page, with **Close**. An item's address (`#/photos/<id>`) can be bookmarked or sent to another admin.
- **Drafts.** What you change is a draft: the page's map previews it at once and nothing is written until **Save**. Save writes one change, stamped with your account; the public map follows within about a minute. **Discard changes** puts the saved version back. Leaving the page, signing out or reloading with unsaved changes asks first (**Keep editing** or **Discard**); switching the language keeps the draft. A field with a problem says so under it, and Save stays off until it is corrected (and says why); a number (a year, a latitude) counts as you type it and says its problem once you leave the field, so a half-typed year does not flash an error.
- **Another admin saved the same item meanwhile**: the editor names them and the time; the fields only they changed are merged in, and for the fields you both changed you choose to keep yours or take theirs. An item deleted meanwhile cannot be saved; History restores it.
- **Every change is kept in History** (below) and can be reverted with one click.
- **Before the Mapbox card is done** (no admin token yet), the editors' map box says so and links the card; every field and the list of positions still work.

### Home

What the city holds and what waits for you (photos whose rights are to be checked, unpublished photos, running or failed jobs), each a link to its page, one chip per Setup area and the public map's state. **What needs you**, first, lists only what waits for a person now, each a link to where it is done: images to approve, role requests, suggestions, and the photos, places and stories an uploader sent that wait in **Review** (an uploader is never shown or asked these counts). While a count is being read its item shows an ellipsis, and a count that could not be read shows a dash: the list never says *Nothing needs you now* while it does not know. Once Setup is complete the admin opens here (`docs/research/DECISIONS-for-review.md` §41c). **Not on the public map** lists every published item the public map left out, with what to correct and a link to its editor.

### Photos

- **Add photos**, or drop the files on the list: JPEG, PNG, WebP and TIFF up to 60 MB. A bigger file, or a file of another kind, is refused in the page before anything is uploaded. Three files upload at a time, each with its progress; an interrupted upload resumes, and **Retry** starts it again.
- **Filled for you**: the title from the file name; the place, year and direction from EXIF for the photos you have not touched yet (a place outside the city is ignored); the author, source, licence and rights holder of your previous batch ("New photos get: …"). A photo without a place goes to your last pin (or the city's centre) and is marked **Check the place** until you move its pin or choose **Confirm place**; bulk Publish skips it meanwhile. After a reload, EXIF values not applied yet show as suggestions with **Apply**.
- **Filters**: All, Unpublished, Processing, Refused, Rights to check; **Search by title or author**.
- **The date**: a year, then if you know them the month and the day: each field appears once the one before it is set, and a date that does not exist is refused with its reason. Visitors read it at that precision. A range of years has no month. A "date as written", when filled in, is what visitors read instead.
- **The source**: its own section in the editor, the https address the photo comes from and its optional name. Visitors see a link under the photo, opening in a new tab; **Author, source and licence** sets it on several photos at once, and an address that is not https leaves each photo as it was and is named as the reason.
- **The editor**: the title and description (Romanian required, English optional: visitors then read the Romanian), the date and the years, author, licence, rights holder, source, permission note, up to 5 https links, rights to check, **Published on the public map**. When a photo opens, the map centres on it (its zoom kept). The place: drag the pin, click on the map, or type the latitude and longitude; **Set direction on the map**, then click on what the camera looked at (with the keyboard: **Point at map centre**); the **Field of view** slider widens the view cone live. A photo uploaded in this session starts with Published on (it is published on save); a photo still processing, refused, or with the licence unknown cannot be published, and the switch says why.
- **Several photos at once**: tick them (or **Select all**), then **Publish**, **Unpublish**, **Author, source and licence**, confirm the rights, or **Delete**. A photo the action cannot change is left as it is and named with its reason (**See why**).
- **Replace image** (in the editor): the new file is processed while the old image stays on the public map, then takes its place in one step. A refused file leaves the old image published and says why; the same file again changes nothing; **Cancel the replacement** stops the upload.
- **Delete photo** asks first; the message after it offers **Undo** for ten seconds, and History restores the photo later (one whose public files were already cleaned up is processed again from its kept original).
- iPhone HEIC photos cannot be converted on the server yet: set the iPhone to Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, or export the photo as JPEG. A photo identical to one already uploaded is marked as a duplicate, with a link to the first one.

Drag the shape by its inside to move it, a corner to move that position, or the dot in the middle of an edge to add a position there; the list of positions follows each drag, and Save writes it. A drag that would make the edges cross stops before they do.
- The category (for a new place, the last one you used), the titles, the description in the Markdown subset with a preview (bold, italics, https links; HTML stays plain text), the years (either may stay empty), up to 5 links, up to 20 linked photos, **Published**. A new place is published on save; **Redraw as …** changes the shape (after asking). A missing field says what to do ("Add a title in Romanian", "Choose a category"), and the note beside **Save** names every missing field. A new city starts with five categories (Building, Street, Square, Church, Monument); in a city with none, the Category field offers **Add the starter categories** or **Add a category**.

- **Export all (GeoJSON)** saves every place (or the selection) as a `.geojson` file. **Import GeoJSON** reads such a file (up to 5 MB) in the page: a preview counts the new, changed, unchanged and refused places, each refusal with its reason (an unsupported shape, an altitude, an open ring, a position outside the city, an unknown category, too many positions); places without a known category take the one you choose; **Import** writes them (at most 5,000 places in all). A file exported and imported again changes nothing.

### Historical layers

- Layers are made by the raster pipeline (M3); until then the page says so. The list shows them in drawing order (the first is drawn on top), with their state and Published; drag one, or use **Move up** and **Move down**.
- **The layer editor**: the label and description (Romanian, English optional), the attribution, year and date, **Shown when the map opens**, **Default opacity**, and four image adjustments, **Black level**, **White level**, **Contrast** and **Saturation**, each read as a signed percentage, with **Reset the adjustments**. Every slider changes the page's map at once; Save publishes the values, and visitors see exactly what you saw. The black level never passes the white level.
- **Link a layer**: a tileset of the city's own Mapbox account, or the https address of a tile server. Another account's tileset (Mapbox's own included) and an address with a key in it are refused before anything is sent. **Check** asks the server for one tile of the city's centre and says what answered ("Tiles answer (image/png)."), or why nothing did (no tiles at this address, not an image, the server not responding); after a failed Check, Link stays off until the address changes. A linked layer starts unpublished and hidden at start. A tile server's address must also be allowed in the site's and the admin's content security policy: the dialog names it.

- **Image and geometry** (a layer made from imagery): **Replace the image** opens the Imagery upload for this layer (choose a GeoTIFF; nothing else is asked, the layer keeps its year); **Georeference again** opens the georeferencer on the layer's current image; **Crop** draws the area to keep on the layer's map, a click per corner (from the keyboard: **Add a corner at the map's centre**), then **Save the crop**; an area off the layer is refused. Each makes a new version: the layer keeps its tiles until that version is published, and the editor shows **A new version is under way: open it** meanwhile. A layer something is being made for cannot be deleted until it is done.

### Historical imagery (M3)

Declassified USGS satellite frames (Corona, Gambit, Hexagon; 1960–1984) become historical layers of the map. The usual way is one click: **Import (free)**, and the server does the rest.

- **Find**: the first time you open **Historical imagery** (and once a month after that), the app searches USGS's three declassified collections over the city's area and ranks the scenes from 0 to 100, the free ones (already scanned) first; each says why in plain words (how much of the city it covers, free or to order, a year the city wants, clouds, detail, a year that already has a layer). Filters: **Free only** (on), **Hide rejected**, the three collections, from and to a year; the map shows the city's boundary and the open scene's footprint only (nearly every frame covers the whole city, so drawing them all would hide the map). Each row shows its USGS preview (the app fetches the ones it has no copy of yet; a preview USGS does not serve shows a broken-image icon, and the scene's page offers **Try again**, as it does when the larger picture could not be made) and a line that tells frames of the same camera and day apart: the USGS id, the camera (forward or aft) and the frame number. **Search USGS again** searches again. When nothing is found, the page suggests every year, all three collections, the scenes to order too, and the city's area in Settings (the search covers only that area).
- **Import (free)** on a scene's card, or on its page (its USGS preview, its facts and every reason of its rank). The server downloads the frame, unpacks and stitches it, finds the city on it, prepares the reference (OpenStreetMap and Sentinel-2, never Mapbox Satellite; of the clear Sentinel-2 dates, the least cloudy one that agrees with the others: a date more than 7.5 m off them is set aside, and the log says so), matches control points on its own, warps and exports it. The download runs on a small machine and the processing on a larger one, by themselves. The version's page shows each step as it runs (the download with the bytes received of the total, "1.23 of 3.4 GB"), the log, and **Cancel** (asked first; Mapbox's own processing cannot be stopped). USGS under maintenance makes it wait, not fail.
- **See what you approve**: a version's page shows the image itself on the map, at full detail, as the public map will: pan and zoom as you like. The slider fades it over the basemap to compare, **Actual pixels** goes to one image pixel on one screen pixel (past that its pixels stay square, so you see where the detail ends), and on a phone **See the image** brings the map back from the card below it. The city's outline is drawn, and what the image misses of the city is shaded, with its share in words. A version with no tiles yet says so and shows a small picture until they are made; if they do not come, **Jobs** shows their job with Retry. A version that will never get them (rejected, ended, or in a city that publishes through Mapbox) says so instead. After an hour on the page the image's address runs out and a new one is fetched on its own. Work's waiting rows each show a small picture of their version.
- **What publishing costs**: by default a layer's tiles go to your own public bucket, not to Mapbox, and the approval card says so with this image's size: Mapbox charges nothing for it; storage is a fraction of a cent a month for each layer; visitors' traffic counts toward Google's free allowance, then Google bills it. **Nothing stops the site if traffic grows: the budget alarm only pauses processing.** Settings → **Historical imagery** → **Publish through Mapbox**, off by default, keeps Mapbox's way for a city that prefers it (or whose project refuses public buckets); the two points below about compute units are that way's. Layers already at Mapbox stay there until a new version replaces them.
- **Publishing**: when the checkpoint error (control points left out of the fit) is under the city's threshold, 15 m by default, it is published without another click. Otherwise the approval card shows the fit and checkpoint errors, the pairs, the Mapbox compute units it costs and those left this month, with **Approve and publish** and **Reject** (asked first). An uploaded GeoTIFF that carries its own georeference always waits for Approve: its error cannot be measured.
- **How the frame was found**: nothing needs placing for a frame the app can find. Its page says how it was found (automatically, or from a neighbouring frame of the same day), how many areas of it agree with the Sentinel-2 reference, the measured error and how much of the city it covers. A frame the app could not prove says "Not found: place a few rough points, the rest is automatic": a few rough points in the georeferencer are enough, the app refines them.
- **Lined up with your other photos**: a frame the app found is also lined up with the photos of the city you already accepted that overlap it, at the streets and fields they share. Those photos stay exactly where they are: nothing you published moves. Its page says which years it was lined up with and at how many shared points; a photo whose streets sat far from the others (more than 6 m) is named and left out, so one photo published wrong cannot pull the next. Lining the older photos up with the new one is your choice: it would change their published maps. A photo placed only by your own points is never used to line up another.
- **Why a good frame still waits**: under the threshold, a frame still waits for **Approve** when it covers under 90 % of the city (add a frame of its date, or approve it anyway) when the automatic check does not cover all of it: it did not prove the frame (your own points always wait for you), a check was over 100 m off, or its agreeing areas span too little of it. The card says so. Expect many frames to wait for that one click: the measured error of a whole frame often sits near 15 m.
- **Over the month's free compute units** (20 a month; a publish costs by area and zoom): the card offers three ways out, each saying what it means: **Free: upload the file in Mapbox Studio** (1. **Download the file**, 2. open Mapbox Studio and upload it as a tileset, 3. **I uploaded it**: the app finds the tileset by its name), **Wait for next month**, or **Publish now, at a cost of about X USD** (asked first; never past Mapbox's 24 tilesets).
- **Too few matching points**: the import waits for yours ("Reliable points the automatic georeferencing found: N, too few"): **Open the georeferencer**.
- **Locate again**: on the page of a USGS frame waiting for points, it searches for the frame's place again with the pipeline as it is today, so a frame the search refused may now be found. What it downloaded and unpacked is kept. It is not a new import, so it does not count against the day's import limit, and it starts no new download; it is free in attempts too: like your points, it always gets a run of its own. A frame whose crop failed offers it as well, before you import it anew, as long as it has attempts left (like Try again). After an update of the imagery pipeline, every frame waiting for points searches again by itself, once (a few a city every five minutes), except one you are placing points on: it waits until you save, or two hours after your last point. New points and crops never count against the day's import limit either. If the working files (14 days) and the USGS delivery (7 days) have both expired, the page says so: add points, or cancel the frame and import it again. A frame moved to the other place (computer or cloud) after it started waiting left those files behind: move it back to locate again, or add points.
- **A file too large for Mapbox**: the approval card says so and offers the one way that works, the free Studio upload of the smaller copy. **At Mapbox's 24 tilesets**: neither paying nor waiting frees a place; delete a layer you no longer need (**Open Layers**) or use Studio.
- **When a run dies** (killed for its memory, for instance), the page says so within about ten minutes; a frame that did not fit in the memory offers **Try again**.
- **When something fails**, the version's page offers what cures it: **Try again** when another run can help (three runs and six publish starts at most), **Open the Mapbox step of Setup** when Mapbox refused the token or its scopes, **Upload another file** when the server refused your upload, and **Dismiss** (asked first) to take a failed version out of **Waiting for you**. **Cancel** works until Mapbox is asked to publish.
- **The georeferencer**: the scan and the reference map side by side (on a phone one at a time, with **Scan** and **Map**; half a pair shows the other pane by itself). Click a place on the scan, then the same place on the map, or by keyboard: move the scan with the arrow keys and the map to the same place, then **Add at the centres**, and again for the next pair; a pair chosen in the table moves with the arrow keys (Shift: ten times further), **Esc** lets it go and **Delete** removes it; every pair refits at once: the table of pairs (each with where it came from, **in the fit** or kept as a checkpoint, and its error in metres; sorted by pair or by error), the fit and checkpoint errors with a warning over the threshold, the transforms the pairs allow (affine from 3, second-order polynomial from 6, thin-plate spline), and the warped preview on the map. A version opens on its own figures, the ones its page shows, and the preview shows the map it publishes (for a KH-9 panoramic frame, the camera fitted on every matched tile); once you change a pair, a note says the figures and the preview are a new fit through your pairs, which Save makes the version's map. Pairs that lie on one line are refused. **Save and georeference** continues the waiting import, or makes a new version of a published layer.
- **Upload**: **Upload a GeoTIFF** (up to 8 GB; the page reads its first bytes and refuses anything that is not a TIFF before sending it). A name with a year ("Plan 1911.tif") starts the upload at once, otherwise the page asks for the year. A large upload goes on after the connection drops. A GeoTIFF with its own georeference goes to the approval card; a scan without one waits for points in the georeferencer.
- **Before USGS approves your downloads** (24–48 business hours after the M2M request): the ranked results show, Import is off, and the page says why with the way to Setup's USGS step. A token USGS refuses links to the same step.
- **Work (n)** counts everything Work lists, live: the imports running, the versions waiting for you and the open orders.
- Limits: 10 imports a day and 2 running at once, per project.
- **Paused by the budget guard**: when the month's Google Cloud cost reaches the budget, imports, retries, the georeferencer's saves, crops and publishes stop (the public map never does). **Historical imagery** and **Jobs** say so with the cost and the budget, and **Resume processing** (asked first) starts them again for the rest of the month; next month's alert pauses them again.

### A whole-city layer from one date's frames (M8)

A KH-9 frame often holds only part of the city. Frames of one date, from one camera, can be joined into one layer. The design: `docs/research/M8-join-interfaces.md`.

- **Whole-city dates**, above Find's list: each date whose frames together cover more of the city, with its frames, about how much of the city they cover (an estimate, so "about"), how many must be downloaded, and **Join frames**. Summer dates come above winter ones: a winter date says it may need points. A frame that would have to be bought is never chosen for you; the row says how many and links its order card. **Add {year} to target years** appears when the year is not one of the city's.
- **The offer**: the page of a frame that leaves part of the city out says how much it covers and how much it would with its date's others, with **Join frames** (also on a scene not imported yet). One click, then the join's page opens.
- **The join's page**: its frames with their states. Frames not imported yet are imported first, and the join waits for them. A frame that needs points waits for yours: its row leads to the georeferencer, and the join goes on by itself after you save them. A frame in a join leaves **Waiting for you** unless it needs points.
- **What the join does**: it measures each seam between two frames and moves them by what was measured, the less accurate frame more, fading to nothing 6 km from the seam. If the measure says a correction did not help, it is not applied. Then it evens the tone along the seam and makes one image.
- **The report** on the approval card: how much of the city it covers (measured now, not estimated), each seam's error before and after, or that it could not be measured, the largest shift, and the worst frame's own error. **A join closes the gaps between frames; it does not improve where they sit on the map.** Frames that add nothing are named as left out. If the city is not covered well, the card offers **Add {frame} and join again**, which downloads nothing a second time.
- **Publishing**: without a click only when auto-publish is on and every limit holds: the worst frame's error and the largest shift within the city's threshold, at least 3 checkpoints, every seam measured, corrected and at most 15 m, and at least 90 % of the city covered. Otherwise **Approve and publish** or **Reject**, with the same compute-unit choices as any layer.
- **While a join holds a frame**, that frame cannot be approved, rejected, re-georeferenced, cropped, deleted or cancelled: reject or cancel the join first. A frame a join ever named never publishes on its own, also once that join ended: it waits for your **Approve**, or for **Join frames**. A rejected, cancelled or failed join ends alone: its frames stay as they were, and joining them again downloads nothing.
- **Limits**: a join counts as one job plus one for each frame still to import, against the 10 a day. At most 8 frames, one date, one camera. A joined layer cannot be cropped or re-georeferenced, and a published join cannot take another frame: delete the layer and start again. To go on without a frame, cancel the join and join the rest.
- **The computer it runs on**: a join reads each frame's whole scan, so it needs disk room and memory; **Run on my computer** may be the only practical place for a whole-city join. How long it takes on real frames has yet to be measured.

### Buying a scan, suggestions, assists and notifications (M4)

- **A frame USGS has not scanned yet** (Free only off, chip **Must be ordered from USGS**) has the **Buy this scan** card on its page: the cost (35 USD for one frame: 30 USD a frame and 5 USD an order, paid to USGS directly, never through this app), the four steps in EarthExplorer and **Order in EarthExplorer**, which opens the scene's own page there in a new tab. Buy it there: sign in (**Login**, top right), click **Order** at the bottom of the page, keep On-Demand on the order page, choose **7 Micron** and click **Submit Product Selections**, then fill in USGS's form and pay 35 USD by card on pay.gov (keep the confirmation number USGS shows at the end); come back and click **I ordered it**: that is the flow's second and last input (the message after it offers **Take the mark back**). A frame USGS already offers cannot be marked ("This frame is already scanned: import it for free, no order needed."); one USGS scanned while you were buying turns free on its page, and a message says so. Until USGS approves your account's downloads, both cards say the daily check waits for that approval, with a link to Setup's USGS step.
- **After I ordered it, nothing is asked of you**: the server asks USGS once a day (**Check now** asks at once, at most once a minute), reminds you on days 7 and 12 to pay if you have not (USGS deletes unpaid orders after 14 days), imports the scan the moment USGS delivers it (delivery takes three to four weeks after payment), georeferences it and publishes it under the city's threshold, exactly like a free import. An order not delivered within 90 days becomes **Never delivered**, with **Order it again** and **Take the mark back** (if you no longer want it). **Take the mark back** also undoes a mark made by mistake. The times of the checks are in your own time zone. The scene's card shows a short chip (Ordered, Ordered: paid?, Delivered, Importing, Never delivered), and **Work** lists the open orders, the one that needs you first: only an order never delivered counts there (a reminder to pay, once sent, asks nothing more of you if you have paid).
- **Suggested pairs in the georeferencer**: when the automatic georeferencing finds too few pairs it trusts, the less sure ones it found wait in **Suggested pairs**, each with its confidence in words and as a bar, and how far it is from the current fit. **Accept** or **Reject** each (on a suggestion, Enter accepts and Delete rejects, and the next one takes the focus), or **Accept all**; accepted pairs join the table and refit at once. Nothing is saved without **Save and georeference**. On the scan the suggestions are dashed rings; on the map they are flags in their own colour.
- **Title suggestions** (places and photos): under the title, **Suggest a title** asks OpenStreetMap what is at the point (a point you draw on the map, or a photo's pin while its title is still the file's name, such as `IMG 0412`, asks by itself), and each answer is a button **Use the title "…"**. At most 300 places a day are asked for the whole project (OpenStreetMap's rules forbid bulk use). OpenStreetMap is asked at most once a second for the whole project, and every answer is kept for 90 days; the suggestions name their source (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The title stays as you typed it until you choose one, and Save writes it.
- **Romanian and English drafts** (the titles, descriptions and labels of photos, places and layers): **English draft** beside a Romanian text while the English one is empty (or **Romanian draft** beside an English one while the Romanian one is empty; a draft never replaces what you typed) asks Google's Gemini model (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, or `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` when Google no longer offers the first to the project; Setup's server checks name the one in use), on the server, for a translation into the other field, marked "Draft proposed by …: check it before you save." The draft is plain text, cut to the field's length (the page says so), and is never published by itself: only your Save writes it. At most 500 drafts or 0.50 USD a month for the whole project; past that, the page says so until next month. A draft that adds a link the text did not have, or changes an address, is not used (the page says so).
- **Notifications**: what the server did that concerns you (a scan delivered, imported, an order to pay or never delivered, a failed processing, a layer waiting for approval or for points, the budget pausing the processing, a burst of crash reports, USGS approving downloads) reaches every admin's **Notifications** page, newest first, unread ones marked; a click opens the page it is about and marks it read; **Mark all as read** marks the rest. **Notifications on this device** turns on push notifications for this browser (the browser asks you first; refused, the inbox still works); on a computer you share, it belongs to whoever turned it on last, and signing out turns it off for you (so does being removed as an admin). Turning it off when the server cannot be reached leaves it on and says why. **Send a test notification** tries it, and **Notifications on devices** chooses which kinds also show on your devices. No e-mail is sent (it would need a new provider: `docs/research/DECISIONS-for-review.md` §54i).
- **The visitors' swipe compare** (on the public map): the Layers panel's **Compare** opens a second map beside the first, with a divider to drag (or move with the arrow keys); **Compare with** chooses the other side (another historical layer or today's map), and each side keeps its layer's opacity. On a window narrower than 600 px a toggle switches between the two sides of one map. A link copied while comparing reopens the comparison.

### Several cities: the city menu, New city, publishing, roles, budgets and the export (M5)

One project can hold up to 20 cities, each with its own photos, places, layers, settings, admins, budget and public map. An admin of one city sees and changes nothing of another, whatever they try: the rules and every function check the city.

- **The city menu**: an admin of two cities or more, and the project's owner, sees the open city's name in the top bar (on a phone, in the account menu). The menu lists your cities, each with your role in it (**Owner**, **Editor**), the open one marked; choosing another opens it (the page loads again, into its Home). The address names the city (`#/c/<city>/...`), so a copied link opens the same city; an admin of one city sees no menu and no city in the address. A link to a city you do not administer opens your own, with a message saying so. With unsaved changes, the admin asks first.
- **New city** (the project's owner: **New city** at the end of the city menu): the name in Romanian (the id follows it: lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, the city's address on the public site), then **Create the city**: two inputs. Once you stop typing, the app finds the city on OpenStreetMap, draws its rectangle and moves the map there ("Found on OpenStreetMap: …"); adjust it if the city is larger or smaller. A name OpenStreetMap does not know leaves the rectangle to you: drag it over the city on the map (or **Draw the area** and click two corners, or type the four edges: the rectangle appears once all four are there), then **Create the city**. The map's **Zoom in** and **Zoom out** buttons and two fingers on a phone move the map. The rest fills itself in and changes any time in Settings: the start view, a theme colour, the city's budget, the viewer's token (once Mapbox is set up), and once USGS is set up the first search for historical imagery of the area runs within minutes. An id no city may take (a reserved word such as engine) is said at its field, and an id another city has is refused with a free one in it; an area under 0.01° or over 2° on a side keeps Create off, with the reason beside the map. The done screen links Photos, Places and Historical imagery, and the address of its public map once published, `https://<project>.web.app/<id>/`, as a link: no repository step is needed (M6); **Export the city** also gives a copy that runs on any static host.
- **Publishing** (Settings → **Publishing**): a new city is not public yet. Switch **The city is public** on and **Save**: within about a minute the server builds the city's public map data; switched off and saved, it takes it down. The section names the city's public address, `https://<project>.web.app/<id>/`, beside the first city at the root, once the map is published (Setup i): the site's generic city page reads any published city, with no repository step. A city's own site on GitHub Pages (`npm run site:add`, a developer's step) stays optional.
- **Admins per city**: Admins lists the open city's admins and invitations, each with its role. A city's owner invites (the address, then the role: **Editor** unless changed; the link is copied at once), removes access, and changes a role (**Make … owner of …?**, then **Change the role**); an editor sees the list only, and why. The project's owner is named as such. A city always keeps at least one owner.
- **The city's budget** (Settings → **The city's budget**): the city's share of the project's budgets, each with what was used so far in its period: Mapbox compute units a month, imagery processing jobs a day, automatic translations a month, title suggestions a day, exports a day. Only the project's owner changes them (one Save for the fields moved); the project's own limits still hold for every city. A new city gets a quarter of the free compute units, three imagery jobs a day, 100 translations a month, 100 title suggestions a day and three exports a day. Bucharest, from before M5, has only the project's limits: the card says so and shows its fields empty, and a field you leave empty saves at its maximum, so the one limit you set is the only one.
- **Export the city** (Settings → **Export the city**, a city's owners): **Export**, then **Download** once the archive is ready (two inputs): a `.zip` with everything the city needs to run on any static host, without Firebase: the page, the engine, `city.json` (the published map, without the project's analytics ids), the photos, a README and `RIGHTS.md`. **Include the USGS scans** adds the scans of the USGS layers (public domain); other layers keep their tile references. The card names how many photos are licensed for this map only (`RIGHTS.md` lists them: ask their rights holders before you publish the copy elsewhere), the archive's size and SHA-256, and until when it can be downloaded (seven days). Publish the city first; an editor sees the card read-only.

### Run on my computer and the free tiers (M7)

Imagery jobs can run on your own computer instead of Google Cloud, which costs nothing but your computer's time and Docker. The design and its security review: `docs/research/M7-local-worker.md`.

- **What you need**: Docker (Docker Desktop on Windows and macOS, Docker Engine on Linux) and about 20 GB free for the work folder. Nothing else: no Google Cloud tool, no key, no token.
- **Use this computer** (nothing to copy or paste): the **Your computer** panel on **Jobs** (and on Setup's imagery pipeline card) has **Use this computer**. Pick the system if it is not yours already (**Linux**, **macOS**, **Windows**) and click it: your browser downloads one small file from the map's server (`hcm-worker.sh` on Linux, `hcm-worker.command` on macOS, `hcm-worker.bat` on Windows) that holds no password or code. Your browser may ask whether to keep it: choose **Keep**; it is saved in your **Downloads** folder, as the panel then says. Open it once: on Linux type `sh ~/Downloads/hcm-worker.sh` in a terminal (the panel shows that line with **Copy the line**; a double-click only opens the file as text); on macOS double-click it in Downloads (if macOS refuses it, type `sh ~/Downloads/hcm-worker.command` in Terminal); on Windows double-click it in Downloads (if Windows warns, **More info**, then **Run anyway**). It checks Docker (and opens **Get Docker** if it is missing), then sets up a background service of your own account, without administrator rights: a `systemd --user` service on Linux, a LaunchAgent on macOS, a task at logon on Windows. The service asks the server which imagery pipeline is installed, downloads it (about 0.6 GB the first time) and runs the worker; it starts again with your session and after a reboot. Keep the admin open: it sees the worker on this computer and connects it by itself (**Connecting this computer…**, then **This computer is connected**); the panel then says **Your computer is connected**. Your browser may ask once whether the admin may connect to devices on this computer: allow it. The worker's page at `http://localhost:8765/` shows what it does.
- **Updates and stopping**: when the imagery pipeline is updated (Update the map), the service sees the worker's image is older, downloads the new one and starts it again by itself: nothing to do. **Disconnect this computer** on the panel signs a worker out for good (**Use this computer** again connects it). **Stop using this computer** gives the same file set to remove everything: open it once and it removes the service, the worker and its Docker volume `hcm-worker`.
- **Advanced: start it with a command**: the panel still has the one-line command of before, behind **Advanced** (for a computer where the file cannot run): **Copy the command**, paste it into a terminal once. It carries a one-time pairing code (once, within 10 minutes; the panel renews it while Advanced is open), runs with Docker's own restart and does not update itself: when the pipeline is updated, copy and run it again. `docker rm -f hcm-worker` stops it.
- **What the panel shows**: whether your computer is connected, the job it is working on with its step and how far it got (live), and how its last job ended.
- **The local AI** (`docs/research/M10-local-ai.md`): a frame the automatic search does not find can ask an AI that runs **on your computer**, on its graphics card when it has one, never in a cloud. It only proposes a place: the map's own check decides, and a wrong proposal is refused like any other. Nothing downloads until a frame needs it, then about 10 GB once (30 GB must be free where Docker keeps its images). The panel says **Local AI: graphics card ...**, **processor only**, **comes when a frame needs it**, or **unavailable** and why; a computer set up before this says to use this computer again, for the new starter file. In Google Cloud there is no AI: such a frame waits for your points. The third-party licences: `pipeline/src/hcm_pipeline/matcher/NOTICE.md`.
- **Where a job runs**: the panel's choice applies to the city: **Run on my computer when it is connected** (the default: a new job goes to your computer if it was seen in the last three minutes, else to Google Cloud), **Always on my computer**, or **Always in Google Cloud**. On an unfinished version that is not running, **Run on my computer** or **Run in Google Cloud** moves that job (and remembers the choice for the city). A job chosen for your computer waits with **Waiting for your computer** until the worker takes it; if the worker stops in the middle, the job waits again and goes on from its last finished step. If no computer comes for 15 minutes, a notification says so and the version offers **Run in Google Cloud instead** (one click).
- **What stays on your computer**: the whole USGS delivery and the cut frames never leave it; only the city's crop and the results (the previews, the reference pictures, the georeferenced file) are uploaded, each through an address that works for 15 minutes and for that one file.
- **If Docker says "denied"** when it downloads the image, your project's organisation does not allow the image to be public: run `gcloud auth configure-docker us-central1-docker.pkg.dev` once (it needs the Google Cloud CLI), then the same command. Without the image's fingerprint (a pipeline installed before this release), the panel asks you to update the imagery pipeline first.
- **Cloud Run's free tier**: the panel shows this month's use of the jobs run in Google Cloud, as an estimate (the run's minutes times its machine's size) against the 180,000 vCPU-seconds and 360,000 GiB-seconds Google gives each month. A job whose run in Google Cloud would pass that waits for you: its page shows the estimate, **Run in Google Cloud anyway** and **Run on my computer**; a job the server made by itself (a delivered order) also leaves a notification.
- **The USGS delivery** is deleted from the bucket once the city's crop exists (a later run downloads it again), and the bucket deletes any left after 7 days.
- **Publishing for free**: a layer whose file is at most 300 MB is published through Mapbox's Uploads API, which uses no compute units (10,000 MB a month are free); a larger one, a layer first published through MTS, or a Mapbox token without the `uploads:read` and `uploads:write` scopes goes through MTS within the month's compute units as before. The version's steps say which way it went: **Free upload to Mapbox** among them, marked skipped when the publish fell back to MTS.

## Costs

- Google Cloud: about $0.5–2 per month; an installation or update uses Cloud Build minutes (the card names them; a month's releases stay within the free minutes) and the imagery pipeline's image about $0.10 a month; an import downloads on 1 vCPU (about $0.09 an hour) and is processed on 4 vCPU (about $0.37 an hour; 8 vCPU, about $0.75, for a KH-9 panoramic frame), mostly within Cloud Run's monthly free tier; Firebase Hosting is free up to 10 GB stored and 360 MB a day; the $5 budget automatically pauses image processing (never the site), and **Resume processing** in Historical imagery or Jobs starts it again for the rest of the month. An imagery job run on your own computer (M7) costs no Cloud Run time at all; the Jobs page shows the month's Cloud Run use against the free tier and asks before a run would pass it.
- Your public bucket (M12, the default): each published layer is one file of about 175 MB (publishing moves it there: it is not kept twice), about half a cent a month at Google's standard price, and visitors' traffic past Google's free allowance is billed by Google. Nothing stops the site if a month goes viral: the budget alarm only pauses processing, so watch your Google Cloud bill.
- Mapbox: free up to 50,000 map loads per month, the basemap's; with **Publish through Mapbox** on, publishing a historical layer costs compute units by its area and zoom, and the app keeps to the month's free 20, otherwise it offers the free Studio upload, waiting for next month, or paying about the amount it names. Since M7 a layer file of at most 300 MB is published through the Uploads API instead, with no compute units (10,000 MB a month free).
- USGS: scanned frames are free; a new scan costs $30 per frame plus $5 per order, paid by you to USGS in EarthExplorer.
- Vertex AI (the Romanian and English drafts): under $0.05 a month at the expected use, and never more than 0.50 USD a month (the app's cap); the budget guard pauses it with the image processing.
- OpenStreetMap's Nominatim (title suggestions) and the notifications: free.
- Another city (M5): its own Firestore documents and photos, its visitors' Mapbox map loads, and one USGS search a month, within its share of the project's budgets; jsDelivr, which serves the engine to a city's own site, and GitHub Pages are free. An export: the task's few minutes of server time and seven days of its archive in the private bucket.
